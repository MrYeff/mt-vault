**Keyes is a strong candidate for your target outcomes.** It directly addresses how individuals experience their place in society. **It is a well-being model, rather than a taxonomy of fundamental needs.**

Its five dimensions are:

|Dimension|What it measures, in plain language|
|---|---|
|**Social integration**|I feel part of a community.|
|**Social acceptance**|I have a positive view of other people, including their differences.|
|**Social contribution**|I believe what I do is useful to society.|
|**Social actualization**|I believe society has potential to develop positively.|
|**Social coherence**|I find the social world understandable.|

These are condensed descriptions of [Keyes’s original model](https://midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/58.pdf). **Acceptance means accepting others—not feeling accepted by others. Actualization concerns society’s potential—not personal self-actualization.**

For your research, this addresses something SDT does not describe as explicitly: **belonging to a wider community, contributing to it, understanding it, and evaluating its people and future.** My assessment is that Keyes deserves a place alongside SDT in your comparison of target outcomes.

There is empirical support, with qualifications:

- **Original validation:** two studies supported the five dimensions and examined relationships with community involvement, neighborhood quality and other measures. [Keyes, 1998](https://midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/58.pdf)
- **Later measurement research:** a five-factor model fitted a student sample reasonably well, but results were mixed in a national adult sample. The five dimensions are useful hypotheses, rather than perfectly established independent axes. [Gallagher et al., 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3865980/)
- **Direct social-media research:** a study used **14 interviews and six months of online-forum observation** to examine older adults’ social-media use during lockdown. It connected maintaining relationships, helping others and participating in communities with Keyes’s dimensions. This supplies qualitative mechanisms; it does not establish causal effects of graph structure. [Wilson-Nash et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/10949968231155156)

**Its main limitation for your “ideal social state” is that it measures people’s appraisals.** Feeling that society is understandable and improving does not establish that their understanding is accurate or that society actually improves. That distinction follows from what the dimensions measure.

For your simulation, I would use this separation:

- **Outcomes:** Keyes’s five dimensions, measured with questionnaires.
- **Explanatory mechanisms:** interactions, recognition, participation, information and beliefs.
- **Network predictors:** reciprocity, exclusion, community membership and access to different people.
- **Validation:** test which predictors actually explain changes in the outcomes.

Those network predictors are **proposed research variables**, not formulas supplied by Keyes. His model gives you a defensible answer to **“What individual social outcomes should I measure?”** It leaves **“Which network structures produce them?”** as your research question.
