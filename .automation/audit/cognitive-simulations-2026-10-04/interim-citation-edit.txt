## Abstract

Reputation is a central element of social communications, be it with human or artificial intelligence (AI), and as such can be the primary target of malicious communication strategies. There is already a vast amount of literature on trust networks and their dynamics using Bayesian principles and involving Theory of Mind models. An issue for these simulations can be the amount of information that can be stored and managed using discretizing variables and hard thresholds. Here a novel approach to the way information is updated that accounts for knowledge uncertainty and is closer to reality is proposed. Agents use information compression techniques to capture their complex environment and store it in their finite memories. The loss of information that results from this leads to emergent phenomena, such as echo chambers, self-deception, deception symbiosis, and freezing of group opinions. Various malicious strategies of agents are studied for their impact on group sociology, like sycophancy, egocentricity, pathological lying, and aggressiveness. Our set-up already provides insights into social interactions and can be used to investigate the effects of various communication strategies and find ways to counteract malicious ones. Eventually this work should help to safeguard the design of non-abusive AI systems.[^r1]

## Research Questions
> Research Questions extracted via LLM
- How do finite-memory, information-theoretic belief updates shape reputation dynamics and emergent group phenomena?[^r2]
- How do specified receiver and communication strategies alter reputation, self-esteem, and group opinion in the simulated settings?[^r3]
- Which defensive receiver behaviors resist the modeled propaganda and deception strategies?[^r4]

## Model
> Model extracted via LLM according to [[Model Notation]]

### What does it represent?

A proof-of-concept agent-based game about perceived honesty. Agents choose conversation partners and a topic, exchange a pair of statements about that topic, and update beliefs about the speakers and topic. “Reputation” is perceived honesty; the model omits other dimensions of human reputation.[^r2]

Friend and enemy lists are the paper-specific directional designation of how an agent has recently been spoken about relative to peer reports. They are model state labels, not validated human relationships or evidence of real friendship.[^r5]

### How does it work?

The compact notation describes shared state and protocol; the mechanics below focus on ordinary/critical reception, with specialized strategies summarized afterward. It is an explanatory condensation, not executable reproduction of all variants.[^r6]

```yml
World:
  agents: [Agent]
  rounds_left: 0..
  event_time: 0..

AgentId: ()
ReceiverStrategy: Naive | Deaf | Uncritical | Critical | Smart
CommunicationStrategy: Ordinary | Strategic | AntiStrategic | Flattering |
                       Egocentric | Aggressive | Shameless | Deceptive |
                       Clever | Manipulative | Dominant | Destructive

Agent:
  honesty: 0..1                   # fixed true frequency x_a
  receiver: ReceiverStrategy
  communication: CommunicationStrategy
  beliefs: [AgentId -> Belief]    # including self
  friends: [AgentId]              # unique directional labels
  enemies: [AgentId]               # unique directional labels
  ToM: [AgentId -> AgentId -> ModelOfOther] # speaker -> agent-topic
  surprise_history: [0..]          # latest nonzero surprises, at most N_k

Belief:
  mu: (-1, MAX_EVIDENCE]
  lambda: (-1, MAX_EVIDENCE]
  # Beta(x | mu+1, lambda+1)
  # mu/lambda are effective honest/lie evidence counts

ModelOfOther:
  believed_belief: Belief
  believed_intention: Belief

Message:
  sender: AgentId
  receiver: AgentId
  topic: AgentId
  content: Belief
  blush_observed: bool

Conversation:
  initiator: AgentId
  partner: AgentId
  topic: AgentId
  first_message: Message
  reply: Message
```

Topics are agents in A, including either conversation participant. Each agent’s true honesty x_a is its fixed probability of an honest communication. The internal belief about any agent c uses I=(mu,lambda), with density Beta(x|mu+1,lambda+1), mean (mu+1)/(mu+lambda+2), and allowed parameters (-1,MAX_EVIDENCE], where MAX_EVIDENCE=10^6 is the numerical cap. Initial beliefs are I0=(0,0) absent a stated alternative.[^r7]

The shared protocol is:

```
MAX_EVIDENCE = 1_000_000
F_BLUSH = 0.1
N_KAPPA = 10
F_CAUTION = 0.3
I0 = (0, 0)
INITIAL_KAPPA = 1

sim_round(mut world: World):
  for a in world.agents:                 # one initiating turn each
    b = choose_partner(a)
    c = choose_topic(a, b)                # c is an AgentId
    J_ab = make_message(a, b, c)
    J_ba = make_message(b, a, c)          # response, same topic
    observe_and_update_self(a, J_ab)
    observe_and_update_self(b, J_ba)
    receive(b, J_ab)                      # only after the pair exchanged
    receive(a, J_ba)
    world.event_time += 2
  world.rounds_left -= 1
```

Each event samples honesty with probability x_a, except strategies that fix this choice. Honest J is the speaker’s current belief I_ac; lies follow its configured deception rule. A lie can cause an observed blush with probability F_BLUSH=0.1, except shameless agents (zero). Speaker self-observation adds one to mu after honesty or lambda after lying. A round has |A| initiators and 2|A| communication events.[^r8][^r9]

The common credibility calculation is:

```
y_J = 1 / (1 + R(d) * (1 / x_ba - 1))
R(d) = product of configured feature likelihood ratios
```

Here x_ba is b’s prior mean belief that speaker a is honest. A blush gives y_J=0; absent blush has lie-to-honest ratio 1-F_BLUSH. A non-blushing disadvantageous self-confession gets y_J=1. Critical receivers also use surprise s_J=KL(J,I_bc), normalized by kappa_b; naive always trust, deaf ignore content, uncritical use basic signals, and smart receivers add Theory-of-Mind expectation matching. Section 4.1 and Table B1 assign confession detection to uncritical as well as critical/smart receivers, while Appendix B.1’s introductory prose and explicit likelihood equations B13 and B22 assign confession only to critical/smart; this source conflict is preserved.[^r10]

For an ordinary/critical receiver b, one message is processed as follows:

```
receive(b, J):
  y = credibility(J, b)               # equation above
  DeltaJ = J.content - ToM[b][J.sender][J.topic].believed_belief
  if DeltaJ.mu < 0 or DeltaJ.lambda < 0:
    DeltaJ = I0                       # discard whole increment
  # Form two joint posteriors over xa and xc from the same prior.
  honest_state: I_ba += (1, 0)
  if J.topic != J.sender:
    honest_state: I_bc += DeltaJ
  else:
    honest_state: I_ba += DeltaJ
  lie_state: I_ba += (0, 1)             # leave topic belief unchanged
  exact_posterior = y * honest_state + (1-y) * lie_state
  stored_product = argmin KL(exact_posterior || product_beta(I_ba, I_bc))
  update ToM, friends/enemies, surprise history
```

If the topic equals the speaker, the honest evidence and topic increment merge in I_ba. The stored product-Beta parameters minimize KL(exact posterior || product-Beta approximation); this cannot retain non-Beta shape or dependence between speaker honesty and topic honesty. ToM, friendship labels, and surprise history update after the compressed belief update.[^r11]

Theory of Mind stores separate estimates of another’s belief and intended message: I_bac ← y_J J+(1-y_J)I_bac; I^e_bac ← (1-y_J)J+y_J I^e_bac. The critical receiver’s surprise scale starts at kappa=1; it keeps the latest N_KAPPA=10 nonzero surprises and sets kappa=median(history)/sqrt(pi), initializing the history consistently with kappa=1. For reports about b itself, compare that speaker’s latest expressed honesty with the median last report from peers: above means friend, below enemy, tie leaves the lists unchanged. These are directional, relative labels.[^r12]

For a targeted lie to a friend or enemy, the sender starts from I_abc, its estimate of receiver b’s belief about topic c, and chooses direction D=(1,0) for a friend or D=(0,1) for an enemy. It samples target normalized surprise S_J from the honest-message surprise distribution, then numerically searches alpha >= 0 so KL_xc(I_abc + alpha D, I_abc) = kappa_a * F_CAUTION * S_J. Here F_CAUTION=0.3 and kappa_a substitutes for the receiver’s unknown surprise scale. A neutral topic gets a white lie J=I_abc: this hides the sender’s actual belief without shifting the recipient’s stated belief.[^r17]

Configured strategies alter the shared protocol; they are fixed personality settings, not learned behaviors. Ordinary selects other partner and any topic uniformly; it lies positively about friends, negatively about enemies, and gives white lies about neutral agents. Strategic weights partners by perceived honesty; anti-strategic by 1−perceived honesty. Flattering makes the partner the topic and lies positively; egocentric chooses self with probability 1/2, otherwise chooses uniformly from all agents (net self probability (1+1/|A|)/2); aggressive selects an enemy topic; shameless never blushes; deceptive always lies. Clever = smart receiver plus deceptive communication. Special compositions are manipulative = clever + flattering + anti-strategic; dominant = clever + egocentric + strategic; destructive = clever + strategic + aggressive + shameless. Destructive agents choose reputed partners through the strategic rule and enemy topics through the aggressive rule.[^r13]

### What can it answer?

The simulations explore whether these stipulated mechanisms can produce echo chambers, self-deception, deception symbiosis, opinion freezing, propaganda effects, and changes in reputation, self-esteem, friendships, and social chaos. They do not establish that these patterns arise in people or platforms.[^r14]

The receiver-comparison runs use 300 conversation rounds; the default intrinsic honesty vector is (x_red,x_cyan,x_black)=(0.27,0.80,0.97). In the tested propaganda setup, a smart receiver eventually identifies exaggerated propaganda and helps other agents distrust it, while critical receivers remain more vulnerable as propaganda inflates their surprise scale; honest cross-communication mitigates impact in some runs (Sections 6.1.1 and 6.2.4–6.2.5, 14–15). Across the three-agent, 100-run ensembles, manipulative agents most often achieve the highest relative reputation within small groups, while dominant agents most often reach the highest absolute reputation; in larger groups destructive agents gain higher average reputation than manipulative/dominant, though the latter remain more effective at reaching the very highest values (Sections 6.4 and 7.3, 26–27).[^r18]

The results are conditional on the chosen group composition and fixed parameterization. The authors describe the dynamics as stochastic and chaotic; single runs are illustrative and the strategy ranking is not a general proof. They show selected four- and five-agent checks, but do not broadly vary the population’s honesty or strategy mix.[^r15][^r18]

### Where does it apply?

This is a deliberately minimalist, ad-hoc proof of concept with fixed character strategies, a single topic type (honesty of an agent), small networks, and simplified cognitive state. The authors say assumptions and strategies need revision, calibration against real-world data, larger networks, and richer language/memory. It should be read as a framework for exploring mechanisms under stated rules, not as validated human psychology or an operational detector for deception.[^r16]
