## Abstract

Data generators have been heavily used in creating massive trajectory datasets to address common challenges of real-world datasets, including privacy, cost of data collection, and data quality. However, such generators often overlook social and physiological characteristics of individuals and as such their results are often limited to simple movement patterns. To address these shortcomings, we propose an agent-based simulation framework that facilitates the development of behavioral models in which agents correspond to individuals that act based on personal preferences, goals, and needs within a realistic geographical environment. Researchers can use a drag-and-drop interface to design and control their own world including the geospatial and social (i.e. geo-social) properties. The framework is capable of generating and streaming very large data that captures the basic patterns of life in urban areas. Streaming data from the simulation can be accessed in real time through a dedicated API.

## Model
> Model extracted via LLM according to [[Model Notation]]

### What does it represent?
The paper specifies a configurable agent-based data-generation framework with agents, an environment, model components and outputs such as check-ins, transactions and meetings. It supplies an architecture and behavior-building primitives rather than one fixed urban behavior model.[^r3]

```yml
Agent:
  needs: [Need]
  beliefs: [Belief]
  characteristics: [Characteristic]
  location: Location
  behaviors: [Behavior]

Behavior:
  trigger: Trigger
  actions: [Action]
  priority: 0..
  goal: Goal

Framework:
  component_library: [ModelComponent]
  model_builder: ModelBuilder
  simulator: Simulator
  environment: GeoSocialEnvironment
```

### How does it work?
A trigger fires when an internal or external condition is met (example: hunger when food level falls below a threshold). It starts a behavior composed of actions; actions proceed until their goal condition, and may pass outputs to the next action. The modeler defines priority when multiple behaviors compete. The Model Builder encodes selected library components; the Simulator decodes the model, creates agents, executes them and writes generated events/data.[^r3]

### What can it answer?
It can generate large geo-social pattern-of-life datasets and stream outputs through an API; the resulting behavior depends on model components chosen by the researcher.[^r4]

### Where does it apply?
It applies to customizable urban trajectory and geo-social data generation. The paper's four-page framework description does not define a universal trigger priority or individual choice equation; both are modeler-specified.[^r3]
