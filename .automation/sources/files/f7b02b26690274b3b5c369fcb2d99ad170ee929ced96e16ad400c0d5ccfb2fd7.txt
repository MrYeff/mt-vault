## Abstract

In complex social systems such as those of many mammals, including humans, groups (and hence ego-centric social networks) are commonly structured in discrete layers. We describe a computational model for the development of social relationships based on agents' strategies for social interaction that favour more less-intense, or fewer more-intense partners. A trust-related process controls the formation and decay of relationships as a function of interaction frequency, the history of interaction, and the agents' strategies. A good fit of the observed layers of human social networks was found across a range of model parameter settings. Social interaction strategies which favour interacting with existing strong ties or a time-variant strategy produced more observation-conformant results than strategies favouring more weak relationships. Strong-tie strategies spread in populations under a range of fitness conditions favouring wellbeing, whereas weak-tie strategies spread when fitness favours foraging for food. The implications for modelling the emergence of social relationships in complex structured social networks are discussed.

## Model
> Model extracted via LLM according to [[Model Notation]]

### What does it represent?
Agents have directed trust/relationship histories, choose social partners by one of four strategies, and trade social turns against foraging turns. A response may cooperate or defect.[^r3]

```yml
Agent:
  strategy: FavourFew | MediumTies | FavourMany | Staged
  trust_to: [AgentId -> 0..]
  interaction_history: [Interaction]
  resources: 0..

Parameters:
  COMPRESSION_RATE: 0..
  WANING_RATE: 0..
  DEFECT_RATE: 0..1
  FORAGE_SOCIAL_RATIO: 0..
```

### How does it work?
Each run has 2,000 cycles in the reported simulations; each agent receives a turn. The forage/social ratio selects a forage turn (resource increment) or social turn. On a social turn, the agent reads its relationship history and selects an alter probabilistically: initially a stranger at random, then strategy-biased by past successful interactions. A cooperating response raises directed trust; defection lowers it and increments defect history. Trust changes are compressed progressively at high trust (log-ratio rule), then wanes each turn, with stronger ties waning less. The responder's defection probability, waning and strategy composition are experimental inputs.[^r3]

### What can it answer?
The simulations measure trust, each ego's ranked relationship strengths and strong/medium/weak tie counts; tie strata are defined by upper, middle and lower trust terciles. The authors compare those patterns and strategy spread under fitness conditions.[^r4]

### Where does it apply?
It applies to the paper's trust-mediated social/foraging model. The article refers to the earlier 2012 paper for the exact partner-search probability functions and trust formula; those equations are not re-derived here.[^r3]
