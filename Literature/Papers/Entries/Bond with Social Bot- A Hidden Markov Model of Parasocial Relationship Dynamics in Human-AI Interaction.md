## Abstract

With the advancement of large language models (LLMs), modern AI systems have developed sophisticated conversational and social interaction capabilities. Platforms increasingly deploy these systems not only for functional task completion but also for social engagement, such as providing emotional support and conducting casual conversations with users. This expanding social role raises a critical operational question for platforms: how should AI responses be configured to cultivate relational bonds between users and the bot, and by extension, between users and the platform? We address this question by modeling the dynamic evolution of users' parasocial relationships with an AI bot as a function of the bot's response characteristics, which we treat as controllable operational design parameters. Drawing on the Computers Are Social Actors (CASA) framework and parasocial relationship theory, we develop a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) where users' latent parasocial relationship states govern their observed interaction choices and AI response characteristics drive transitions between states. We estimate the model on a longitudinal panel of user interactions with Grok, the built-in AI bot on X (formerly Twitter). We find that greater anthropomorphism and social intelligence in the bot's responses significantly increase the probability that users transition to higher parasocial relationship states, while greater linguistic complexity and a more analytical style significantly reduce it. These effects are most pronounced among users in the low parasocial relationship state, suggesting that the bot's communicative characteristics are especially consequential before meaningful relational bonds have formed. Design simulations identify social intelligence as the most influential operational lever for fostering sustained social engagement. Our findings provide implications for calibrating AI response strategies to cultivate users' relational engagement.
