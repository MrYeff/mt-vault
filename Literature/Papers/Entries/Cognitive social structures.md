## Abstract

There are problems within the area of network analysis that can be fruitfully explored with cognitive social structures (CSS). Such structures can be modeled as three-dimensional (N × N × N) network structures. A definition of such structures is presented, along with a review of some of the problems CSS might address. Three types of aggregations of CSS - Slices, Locally Aggregated Structures (LAS), and Consensus Structures (CS) - are proposed to reduce CSS to a tractable two dimensions for analysis. As an illustration, the CSS of a management team of a small manufacturing firm is analyzed comparing all three types of aggregations.

## Model
> Model extracted via LLM according to [[Model Notation]]

### What does it represent?
A cognitive social structure is a three-dimensional set of directed tie reports: each perceiver reports relations among the members of a bounded group. The paper defines individual slices and proposes locally aggregated and consensus structures. (Sections 2–3.)[^r1]

### How does it work?
- For each perceiver k, a slice records that person's reported network. (Section 3.2, p. 116.)[^r2]
- A locally aggregated structure (LAS) is based on reports by the dyad's own members: the row-dominated form is R′ᵢⱼ=Rᵢⱼᵢ (i's report), and the column-dominated form is R′ᵢⱼ=Rᵢⱼⱼ (j's report). (Section 3.2, p. 116.)[^r2]
- Consensus structures instead aggregate reports from all perceivers. These are measurement and aggregation procedures, not temporal simulation rules. (Section 3.2, p. 116.)[^r2]

### What can it answer?
The framework supports comparison of reported network perceptions and their aggregations; the paper illustrates the methods with a management team from a small manufacturing firm. (Sections 3–4.)[^r3]

### Where does it apply?
It applies to bounded groups where respondents can report ties among group members. The case illustration does not establish general accuracy across settings. (Section 4.)[^r4]
